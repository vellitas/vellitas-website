Methodology

Focused observation. Explainable confidence. Minimal impact.

Vellitas observes the evidence an internet-facing service intentionally presents to a client. The assessment is designed to be narrow, repeatable, and efficient.

01

What we observe

Certificate chain, subject and SANs, issuer, validity, fingerprints, public-key and signature characteristics, TLS versions and cipher support, chain completeness, revocation signals, DNS and reverse DNS, IP/network owner, observed location, and changes across time.

02

Standard cadence

A full baseline at onboarding; daily review of changed or newly discovered endpoints; a weekly portfolio reassessment; monthly reporting; quarterly scope confirmation; and an on-demand retest after remediation. Contracted service levels control notification timing.

03

Confidence scoring

Confirmed findings are reproduced directly. High confidence combines direct evidence with corroborating context. Moderate confidence identifies a real signal with unresolved ownership or attribution. Low-confidence candidates are held for validation rather than presented as remediation work.

04

What we do not access

No credentials, private keys, user accounts, application databases, encrypted payloads, or internal networks are accessed unless a customer separately and explicitly authorizes a defined test. Vellitas does not exploit defects, guess passwords, or perform availability testing.

Responsible scanning

One focused handshake, then back off.

Vellitas makes rate-limited connections, captures only the technical evidence required for the assessment, closes the connection, retries conservatively, and pauses when a service shows signs of stress. It does not collect page content as part of the certificate assessment.

Third-party exposure

Your vendors extend your attack surface.

A supplier does not have to store your data to create risk. Remote access, trusted software, embedded code, identity services, and connected infrastructure can turn a weakness at one organization into an attack path into another.

Small vendor → large enterprise

Target

A U.S. Senate staff analysis reported that attackers obtained credentials from a small HVAC contractor and used vendor access to enter Target's environment.

Embedded service → payment page

Ticketmaster

The UK regulator found that malicious code inserted into a third-party chatbot could capture customer and payment information from Ticketmaster pages.

Management platform → downstream businesses

Kaseya

Attackers exploited management software used by service providers, ultimately affecting nearly 1,500 downstream organizations, including small businesses.

Vendor assurance should be continuous and proportionate to access—not a questionnaire completed once during procurement.
